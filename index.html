Synopsis

The neglected band program at Skookumchuck High School is not a place of musical excellence, but it is a safe haven for five geeky teenagers — and now, a substitute teacher in the midst of fighting for her failing music career.

Story

Alicia Bisbee, a 25 year old musician taking a "creative hiatus" from her struggling musical duo, arrives at her new small-town substitute teaching gig with nothing but the DIY tour bus she lives out of and a "how to teach" Wiki-How article.

Now in charge of the five oddball students who make up Skookumchuck High's band class, she must figure out how to balance the students' musical incompetence with the jock-y principal's dreams of a majestic American-level marching band.

Set in a run down and underfunded public high school in a small Canadian town, Band Class explores the inherent defiance needed to find joy in being the underdog.

Tone

Band Class is a comedy rooted in bad music, mockumentary-style camera work, and absurd yet touching characters. Its visual style is grounded in the gritty, small-town, athletics-centered public school — bringing a grounded feeling that the larger-than-life characters flourish against.

This show is a refuge for the cringe-y teenagers; where they can be celebrated for all of their awkward mishaps, without needing a make-over-induced redemption arc.

Alicia and the band kids are funny and eccentric despite the world they're in, not because of it.

why band class, why now?

"The idea for Band Class first came to me when I was a dorky teenager myself, frustrated by the portrayal of the teenage experience in film and television, especially the experience of female nerds. I could never find myself or my friends in the hyper-sexualized twenty-something year old women on screen that we were told we should emulate, and I felt like there was something wrong with me because of that.

Now with the continued rise of social media and self-surveillance it is more crucial than ever to not only show the weirdness of young-adulthood but give it its own space to be celebrated.

The other side of Band Class is the story of an underfunded arts program. Save for the lucky few, the "neglected high school arts classes" is all too common of an experience. Which is tragic when you consider the value of these spaces in the notoriously unforgiving high school environment and their role in a society that is increasingly focused on output and productivity.

None of us band students went on to become professional musicians, but it was in this program that I found myself surrounded by kids from all grades who were usually on the outskirts, and who ended up being some of the funniest and most interesting people I crossed paths with in high school. All thanks to this space that allowed us to flourish.

So, this show is a love letter to the arts spaces and the unique kids that fill them, because we need these spaces now more than ever, and it is high time these stories are celebrated as much as any other high school experience.

Plus, who better to tell these stories than a group of young filmmakers who also grew up under the gaze of social media, and who still feel the echoes of humiliation from those fatefully awkward years."

— Kaylin Schober, Writer / Director / Co-Producer

The Team

Kaylin Schober

Writer / Director / Co-Producer

Born and raised on the Sunshine Coast, Kaylin Schober is a Vancouver-based writer and director with a passion for heartfelt comedies. Her work has been shown at the local Run n Gun and Shits 'N Giggles festivals, winning an audience choice award at the latter. Her 2023 thesis film, Retirement Home Daredevil, was nominated for "Best Student Film" at the Leo Awards.

Jenelle Hagerman

Director of Photography / Associate Producer

Jenelle Hagerman is a Canadian filmmaker from Vancouver, British Columbia. She is most notably known for her work in post production and producing on films such as 'Who Am I' (2024) and 'Unscripted' (2023). Her additional work in cinematography has received critical acclaim, winning Best Canadian Short at Montreal Independent Film Festival, establishing herself as an emerging artist in the Canadian independent film category. In 2023, Jenelle completed an Artist Residency, making her directorial debut with her short documentary, 'A Place We Call Home' (2024), which is set to release in fall of 2024.
